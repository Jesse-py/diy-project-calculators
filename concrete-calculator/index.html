How the calculator works

Volume is length × width × thickness, with the thickness converted from inches to feet (divide by 12). A 10 × 10 ft slab 4 inches thick is 10 × 10 × 4 ÷ 12 = 33.3 cubic feet. There are 27 cubic feet in a cubic yard, so that's 1.23 cubic yards before waste. A round pad uses π × radius² × thickness, and a post hole uses the same formula with the hole's diameter and depth.

For bags, the calculator divides the volume (plus your waste allowance) by how much one bag makes, then rounds up. QUIKRETE's data sheet lists these yields:

Tips before you pour

  • Add for waste. Ground is never perfectly flat, forms bow a little, and some concrete gets spilled. 5–10% extra is a common allowance; use more if the base is uneven. Running short halfway through a pour is much worse than having a bag left over.
  • Measure the real depth. If your base is dug 4½ inches deep in places, use that. Half an inch over a 10 × 10 slab is another 4 cubic feet, about 7 more bags.
  • Check the minimum thickness. QUIKRETE says its standard Concrete Mix is for pours 2 inches thick or more.
  • Water. The data sheet calls for about 6 pints (3 quarts) of water per 80 lb bag, so 60 bags is roughly 45 gallons.

FAQ

How many 80 lb bags of concrete are in a yard?

45. One cubic yard is 27 cubic feet, and an 80 lb bag makes about 0.60 cubic feet (27 ÷ 0.60 = 45). With 60 lb bags it's 60 per yard.

How many bags for a 10 × 10 slab?

At 4 inches thick: 33.3 cubic feet, which is 56 bags of 80 lb mix with no extra, or 62 bags with 10% added for waste.

How much concrete per fence post?

A 10-inch hole 3 feet deep holds about 1.6 cubic feet, which is 3 bags of 80 lb mix (with 10% extra). Set the shape to "Post holes" and enter your hole size and number of posts. The post itself takes up some of the space, so you'll have a bit to spare. Planning a whole fence? The fence calculator sizes the holes, subtracts the post, and counts posts, rails, and pickets too.

Does this work for other brands?

Yes, if you know the yield. Most bags print it (for example "yields 0.6 cu ft"). Bag yields for the same weight are similar across brands, but check yours if you're ordering exactly.
