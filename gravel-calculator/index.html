How much does a yard of gravel weigh?

It depends on the rock, the size and mix of the pieces, how wet it is, and how tightly it's packed, so there's no single right number. Two reference points:

  • Bagged gravel: a 50 lb bag of QUIKRETE All-Purpose Gravel (No. 1151, about 3/8 inch stone) is sold as 0.5 cubic feet. That's 100 lb per cubic foot, or 2,700 lb (1.35 tons) per cubic yard. This calculator uses it as the default.
  • Normal-weight aggregate in general: a Penn State civil engineering course table (after Mindess) puts the dry-rodded unit weight of normal-weight concrete aggregates at 1,100 to 1,750 kg per cubic meter, which is about 0.93 to 1.47 tons per cubic yard. Dry-rodded means compacted in a test container; loose stone in a truck weighs less per yard.

Suppliers often bill by the ton, which is weight, while your project is a volume. That's why the supplier's own figure for the product you're buying matters most: if they say a yard of their #57 stone weighs 1.4 tons, enter 1.4.

How the calculator works

Volume is area × depth, with the depth converted from inches to feet. The default 20 × 10 ft area is 200 square feet; at 4 inches that's 200 × 4 ÷ 12 = 66.7 cubic feet. Adding 10% gives 73.3 cubic feet, or 2.72 cubic yards (÷ 27). At 1.35 tons per yard that's 3.67 tons (7,333 lb), or 147 bags of 0.5 cubic feet, rounded up. For a circle, the area is π × radius².

The extra percentage is a judgment call, not a standard. Loose gravel settles when it's raked, driven on, or run over with a plate compactor, and the ground is rarely perfectly flat, so ordering a little more than the bare volume avoids a second delivery. Set it to 0 to see the bare volume.

Bags or bulk?

One cubic yard is 54 bags of 0.5 cubic feet, about 1.35 tons of 50 lb bags. Bags make sense for a small path or a few post bases; for a parking pad or driveway, bulk delivery by the ton is usually far cheaper and saves carrying dozens of bags, but there's often a delivery fee and a minimum load. Compare the total price for your amount.

FAQ

How many tons of gravel do I need for a 10 × 20 area?

At 4 inches deep, 10 × 20 ft is 66.7 cubic feet = 2.47 cubic yards, or 3.33 tons at 1.35 tons per yard. With 10% extra, order about 3.7 tons.

How many cubic yards are in a ton of gravel?

About 0.74 cubic yards (20 cubic feet) at 1.35 tons per yard. Divide 1 by your supplier's tons-per-yard figure: at 1.5 tons per yard, a ton is 0.67 cubic yards.

How many 50 lb bags of gravel are in a yard?

54 bags, if each bag is 0.5 cubic feet (27 ÷ 0.5). Check the volume printed on the bag; it's the number that counts.

Does this work for sand, pea gravel, or river rock?

Yes. The volume math is the same for anything spread to a depth. Only the weight per yard changes, so enter your supplier's figure for tons.
