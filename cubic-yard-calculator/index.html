Cubic Yard Calculator

Lengths in feet, depth in inches. For a circle, enter the diameter (all the way across). For a triangle, the base and the height measured square to it.

Volume

How to calculate cubic yards

Cubic yards = length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (in) ÷ 324. The 324 is 12 inches per foot × 27 cubic feet per cubic yard. If you measured the depth in feet, divide by 27 instead.

Example: a 12 × 20 ft area at 4 inches deep is 12 × 20 × 4 ÷ 324 = 2.96 cubic yards (80 cubic feet). A 10 ft circle at 3 inches is π × 5² = 78.5 square feet, × 3 ÷ 324 = 0.73 cubic yards.

Use the right calculator for your material

The volume is the same whatever you're filling, but each material has its own catch, so the material pages add it:

  • Mulch: recommended depths, top-ups over old mulch, and bag counts.
  • Topsoil: extra for settling, since recommended depths are settled depths.
  • Gravel: tons as well as yards, because bulk stone is often sold by weight.
  • Concrete: bags of premix and a waste allowance, plus post holes.
  • Retaining wall: blocks and caps, plus gravel for the base trench and the drainage zone.

FAQ

How many cubic feet are in a cubic yard?

27. A cubic yard is 3 ft × 3 ft × 3 ft. It's also about 0.765 cubic meters (a yard is exactly 0.9144 m).

How many square feet does a cubic yard cover?

324 divided by the depth in inches: 162 square feet at 2 inches, 108 at 3, 81 at 4, 54 at 6.

How do I convert square feet to cubic yards?

Multiply the square feet by the depth in inches and divide by 324. 500 square feet at 3 inches is 500 × 3 ÷ 324 = 4.63 cubic yards.

How many tons is a cubic yard?

It depends on the material. Gravel and crushed stone are roughly 1.35 tons per cubic yard by the bag figures our gravel calculator uses, but suppliers' numbers vary; mulch is far lighter. Ask the yard for their weight per yard.

Should I round up?

Yes. Some yards sell only in half or whole cubic yards, and ground is rarely perfectly flat. Rounding up to the next half yard is a common cushion; for concrete, add a waste allowance (the concrete calculator adds 10% by default).
