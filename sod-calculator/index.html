Sod Calculator

How much sod to order for a new lawn: square yards with extra for cuts, the number of slabs or rolls, how many pallets that is, what it weighs, and roughly what it costs. Everything runs in your browser.

Measure the lawn as simple shapes and add them up, or measure the whole lot and subtract the house, drive, and beds. Piece and pallet sizes vary with the farm's cutting machine; ask your supplier and change them here.

What to order

How much sod do I need?

Sod is sold by the square yard. Purdue Extension's guide to establishing a lawn from sod says to divide the lawn's square feet by 9 and order about 10% more for measuring mistakes and the pieces you cut away at edges and corners.

Purdue's own example: a lawn of 3,779 sq ft is 3,779 ÷ 9 ≈ 420 square yards, and 10% more makes 462 square yards. That's 1,386 slabs of 18 × 24 in, or 9 pallets of 450 sq ft plus 108 sq ft more (36 slabs). To find the 3,779, Purdue measured the whole lot (6,000 sq ft) and took away the house, driveway, porch, and mulch beds (2,221 sq ft).

How much sod is on a pallet?

Purdue says sod normally arrives on wooden pallets of about 50 square yards (450 sq ft). Slabs are typically 18 × 24 in (3 sq ft), so a pallet holds about 150 of them; rolls are typically 18 in × 6 ft (9 sq ft, about 50 a pallet) or 24 in × 5 ft (10 sq ft, about 45 a pallet). Both vary by farm, so check before you order. Many suppliers sell part pallets, and the price often includes a deposit on each pallet (Purdue: typically under $10).

How much does sod weigh?

About 35 to 40 lb per square yard, so a full 50-yard pallet is roughly 1,750 to 2,000 lb. That's more than many half-ton pickups are rated to carry. The truck bed capacity calculator works out your truck's limit from the door-jamb sticker; for more than a part pallet, delivery is usually simpler and puts the pallets near where you'll lay them.

How much does sod cost?

Purdue's guide lists about $1.50 to $3.00 per square yard for common lawn grasses in Indiana (Kentucky bluegrass and tall fescue $1.50–$3.00, bermudagrass $1.50–$2.50, zoysiagrass $2.00–$3.00), not counting installation. Prices vary with the grass, the farm, the amount, and retail versus wholesale, so enter a local quote above for a real figure. Delivery and pallet deposits are extra.

Before the sod arrives

  • Test the soil and add topsoil if it's thin. Penn State Extension recommends at least 4 to 6 inches of good topsoil under a lawn. The topsoil calculator works out the cubic yards; 6 inches over 1,000 sq ft takes about 18.5.
  • Grade and firm the bed. Purdue: it should be firm enough to walk on with only the top ½ inch loose, and drives and walks should sit about ½ inch above the soil to allow for the soil on the sod.
  • Water the soil a day or so ahead, so it's lightly moist but not muddy. Don't lay sod on dry, hot soil.
  • Schedule delivery for the day you'll lay it. Purdue says sod should be delivered and installed within 24 hours of harvest; it deteriorates the longer it sits on the pallet.

Laying it

Start along a straight edge such as a driveway (or a string line), and on a slope start at the bottom. Butt the pieces tightly without overlapping, and stagger the joints row to row like bricks. Avoid small pieces, which dry out: Purdue's rule of thumb is nothing narrower than 9 in or shorter than 18 in, with trimmed pieces kept on the inside. Roll the lawn lightly, then water it thoroughly, and keep watering daily in the morning until it roots, usually about 10 days (10 to 14 in good weather). To check, lift a corner; new roots are white.

FAQ

Should I round up to whole pallets?

Only if your supplier sells whole pallets only. The calculator shows both: whole pallets plus the leftover in square feet and pieces, and the count rounded up. The 10% extra already covers ordinary cutting, so a full extra pallet is usually more than you need.

When is the best time to lay sod?

Purdue: fall for cool-season grasses such as tall fescue and Kentucky bluegrass, so roots grow before summer; early summer for warm-season grasses such as bermudagrass and zoysiagrass. Sod laid in summer heat or in winter can work but needs much more care with watering.

How do I measure an odd-shaped yard?

Split it into rectangles, circles, and triangles, work out each area, and add them, or measure the whole lot and subtract what isn't lawn. Enter the total with I know the square feet. The cubic yard calculator adds up mixed shapes if you also need soil for them.

Can I lay sod over existing grass?

Purdue's guide prepares a bare, graded bed: control perennial weeds and undesirable grasses first, then grade, add topsoil, and fertilize as a soil test recommends. Sod on top of an old lawn roots poorly.
