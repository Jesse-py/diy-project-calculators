How big is a cord of wood?

A cord is 128 cubic feet of stacked wood: the classic stack is 4 feet high, 4 feet deep, and 8 feet long. That's the legal definition in NIST Handbook 130, the model weights-and-measures rules most states adopt: the wood in a 128 cubic foot space when it's “ranked and well stowed”, meaning the pieces are in a row, touching, parallel, and stacked compactly. It's 128 cubic feet of wood and air, not 128 cubic feet of solid wood.

Any stack works out the same way: length × height × piece length (in feet), divided by 128. A stack 8 feet long and 4 feet high of 16-inch pieces is 8 × 4 × 16/12 = 42.67 cubic feet, which is one third of a cord.

Face cord, rick, and other words for less than a cord

A “face cord” (also called a rick, rank, or short cord) is a stack 4 feet high and 8 feet long, but only one piece deep. How much wood that is depends entirely on the piece length:

That's why NIST Handbook 130 says the terms “face cord,” “rack,” “pile,” and “truckload” shall not be used to sell firewood: sellers are supposed to quote cords and fractions of a cord (or cubic meters). Not every state has adopted that rule, and the words are still common, so convert before you compare prices.

Compare prices per full cord

Divide the price by the share of a cord you're getting. A face cord of 16-inch wood for $150 is one third of a cord, so it costs $450 per full cord. A face cord of 24-inch wood at $200 is half a cord, or $400 per cord: the bigger stack is the better deal even though it costs more. Enter the stack and its price above to do the math for any offer.

How much firewood fits in a pickup truck?

Less than the bed's volume suggests. In 1982 Clemson University foresters loaded four pickups with 18-inch and 24-inch firewood, both thrown in and stacked, level with the sides and above them, then restacked every load on a rack and measured it. Their conclusion: a pickup holds anywhere from less than one fifth to slightly over one half of a cord, and using the box volume to estimate a truck's capacity “will result in an overestimate.” Their 18-inch results:

Two things stand out. Wood thrown in loose fills the bed with a lot of air: a level load of thrown wood restacked to only about 61–62% of the bed's volume. And even neatly stacked wood came to only 80–84% of the bed, because real pieces aren't uniform. The truck calculator above scales those measured shares to your bed. The study's trucks are old, so treat the result as an estimate; the shares were very consistent across the four trucks.

For the example bed (78 × 60 × 20 inches, wheel wells inside), that's 54.17 − 2.8 = 51.37 cubic feet of box, so about 0.32 to 0.34 of a cord stacked level, or about 0.25 of a cord thrown in. A full cord won't fit in an ordinary pickup bed.

Check the weight before you load

Firewood is heavy. The Clemson bulletin warns that a cord of green (unseasoned) hardwood such as hickory or oak weighs over 6,000 pounds, and that even one third of a cord will overload a half-ton pickup. Look up your truck's payload on the tire and loading placard in the driver's door jamb, and use the truck bed capacity calculator to see how much weight that leaves for cargo. Several trips with a partial load beat one overloaded one.

When you buy

  • Ask for the price per cord or per fraction of a cord, and the piece length.
  • Get a ticket. Under NIST Handbook 130 the seller gives you a delivery ticket or invoice for non-packaged wood showing the seller's and your names and addresses, the delivery date, the quantity delivered (and the quantity the price is based on, if different), the price, and the wood's identity.
  • Stack it and measure it. Delivered wood is usually dumped loose; stack it neatly and use the calculator above to check you got what you paid for. If you didn't, your state or county weights and measures office handles firewood complaints.

FAQ

How many face cords are in a full cord?

Three, if the pieces are 16 inches long; two with 24-inch pieces; about 2.67 with 18-inch pieces. Divide 48 (inches in a full cord's depth) by the piece length in inches.

What size is half a cord?

64 cubic feet, for example a stack 8 feet long, 4 feet high, and 2 feet deep, or two rows of 12-inch pieces 8 feet long and 4 feet high.

How many cords are in a 16-foot stack that's 4 feet high?

With 16-inch pieces, 16 × 4 × 16/12 = 85.33 cubic feet, or two thirds of a cord. With 24-inch pieces it's a full cord (128 cubic feet).

Does a cord of split wood hold more wood than a cord of rounds?

A cord is a measure of stacked space, so both are 128 cubic feet. The Clemson bulletin notes that crooked or knotty pieces, thinner pieces, and longer pieces all leave less solid wood in a stack.
